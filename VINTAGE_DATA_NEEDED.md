# Vintage data to collect later

We can use stored professional vintage charts without a live feed. The user will collect spreadsheets or screenshots another time; no action is needed from them now. Once supplied, prepare the data for the existing Vintage Intelligence importer, retain provenance, validate matching, and identify any unreadable or ambiguous entries before using them. Do not invent ratings or treat test fixtures as real chart data.

## Preferred format

An Excel spreadsheet is easiest and least prone to transcription errors. One workbook with separate region tabs is fine; separate files are also fine. Keep the original chart/export layout—there is no need to code, normalize names or prepare the application's import template yourself.

Clear screenshots are also usable. Include the chart headers, years, region labels, source name and rating legend. Capture large charts in overlapping sections so no rows or columns are cut off. Include color/symbol legends if those encode quality, wine type, maturity or missing data. Quality ratings must remain distinct from drinking-status indicators.

## Information needed

- Professional publication or regional authority, such as Wine Advocate, Wine Spectator, Vinous, Decanter, Jancis Robinson or Berry Bros. & Rudd.
- Source URL or a reference identifying the chart and its edition/update date, when shown.
- Vintage years and their original ratings—points, stars, words, letters or other explicitly explained symbols.
- Exact geographic labels, including any subregion/appellation distinctions the chart provides.
- Wine type/style distinctions when provided: red, white, sweet, sparkling, Napa Cabernet, etc. Preserve a general assessment as general rather than guessing a color.
- The rating scale and legend, including the meaning of blanks, dashes, colors and symbols.
- Short professional context notes if useful, such as documented frost or smoke issues. Full articles are unnecessary.

Use data you are permitted to access and use. An authorized export or screenshots of charts you can legitimately view can be prepared for your personal app; access restrictions will not be bypassed.

## Regions to prioritize for the current inventory

1. Burgundy: red/white separately where available; Côte de Nuits, Côte de Beaune and Chablis.
2. Italy: Barolo, Barbaresco, Brunello di Montalcino, Chianti Classico, Bolgheri and broader regional charts where appropriate.
3. Bordeaux: red, dry white and sweet; Left/Right Bank and appellations where available.
4. Rhône: Northern/Southern Rhône and red/white where distinguished.
5. Champagne: vintage Champagne. NV/MV wines do not need a normal vintage rating.
6. California/USA: Napa Cabernet and other applicable regional/style charts.
7. Germany: the regions and styles actually covered by the source.

Collect the vintage years covered by your inventory where possible; the full chart is easier than selecting every wine individually. One reputable source is enough to start. Additional professional sources can improve confidence and reveal disagreement. There is no need to gather every source before starting.

## Processing after the files arrive

Transcribe or parse the supplied charts, preserve original values and references, flag uncertain readings, normalize geography and documented rating scales, and import into the existing app. Validate matches against the private Flickinger reference workbook. Prefer the most specific applicable geography/type, retain broader fallbacks and source disagreement, and leave unsupported or missing information unassessed with neutral treatment. Historical charts can be reused locally; a live feed or frequent updates are unnecessary. New chart editions can be imported later when useful.
